Transparent by design

Know what the site is doing—and what it isn't.

The research index and the consumer habits test are separate systems. Both publish their inputs and limitations.

Research database

How the index is built.

Publication source

Publication records are discovered through NCBI's Entrez Programming Utilities against PubMed. HRD stores bibliographic metadata, publication types, MeSH headings, keywords and source identifiers. It does not republish complete article text or full abstracts.

Clinical trial source

Trial records are retrieved from the ClinicalTrials.gov v2 API. Status, study type, phase, enrollment, conditions, interventions, dates and source links are normalized when available.

Update schedule

A GitHub Action is scheduled twice weekly and can also be run manually. It searches each defined topic, deduplicates records, regenerates statistics and pages, and deploys the refreshed static site.

Precision filter

Topic search queries are followed by a conservative metadata check. A record only keeps a topic membership when the intervention is visible in the title, keywords, MeSH headings, trial title, conditions or intervention metadata. This reduces obvious false positives caused by broad search terms.

What the research index does not do

  • Paper counts are not treated as proof.
  • Research depth is not an efficacy rating.
  • An association is not automatically converted into causation.
  • A registered trial is not treated as a positive result.
  • Automated design labels do not replace risk-of-bias review.
  • The site does not provide diagnosis or treatment advice.

Consumer tool

Healthspan Habits Score methodology.

This is intentionally a simple habit-alignment index, not a validated biological-age model.

What the 0–100 number means

The test contains 10 questions worth up to 10 points each. Higher-point answers align more closely with broad public-health guidance on physical activity, strength training, sleep, diet, tobacco exposure, alcohol misuse, sedentary behavior and social connection. The recovery-routine question is included as a behavioral self-management item, not as a medical risk factor.

The score does not estimate biological age, mortality risk, disease risk, fitness level or life expectancy. It only summarizes the answers you selected against the published rubric visible on the result page.

Why equal weighting?

Equal 10-point categories make the tool easy to understand and audit. HRD is not claiming that each behavior has equal causal importance. A clinically validated weighted risk model would require population-specific modeling, outcome validation and substantially more personal data.

Privacy

The static test does not require an account or email. The most recent result may be saved locally in the visitor's browser. A shared challenge exposes the score in the URL but does not include the person's answers.

Guidance used to anchor the rubric

Physical activity: common adult guidance centers on at least 150 minutes of moderate activity and muscle strengthening on 2+ days weekly.

Sleep: CDC materials state most adults need at least 7 hours and recommend a regular sleep schedule.

Fruit & vegetables: WHO guidance recommends at least 400 g, roughly five portions, per day for people older than 10.

Tobacco: CDC identifies commercial tobacco use as a leading preventable cause of disease and death.

Alcohol: NIAAA defines binge drinking as a pattern typically corresponding to 4+ drinks for women or 5+ for men in about two hours.

Social connection: the U.S. Surgeon General has published an advisory on the health effects of social connection and isolation.

Study-design classification rules

PubMed publication-type labels drive the automated design bucket. Where PubMed does not supply a recognized label, the record is categorized as “Other research.”
