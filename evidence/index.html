Evidence context

Study design is context, not a score.

A randomized trial and an observational study answer different questions and carry different limitations. Our labels make the design visible without automatically declaring the finding “strong” or “weak.”

A
Systematic reviews & meta-analyses

Syntheses of multiple studies. Their usefulness depends on the quality, similarity and completeness of the included evidence.

B
Randomized controlled trials

Participants are assigned to interventions using randomization. Useful for causal questions, but sample size, blinding, adherence and endpoints still matter.

C
Controlled / other clinical trials

Interventional human research that may not be randomized or may use other controlled designs.

D
Observational research

Cohort, cross-sectional and other observational designs can reveal associations, but confounding and selection effects can limit causal conclusions.

E
Reviews, case reports, preclinical & other

Useful for hypotheses, mechanisms, unusual observations and context, but not automatically equivalent to controlled human outcome evidence.

Important: These are design buckets, not a GRADE assessment, risk-of-bias review, or clinical recommendation. A well-conducted study in one bucket can be more informative than a poorly conducted study in another.
