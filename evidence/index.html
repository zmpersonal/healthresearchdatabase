Evidence context

Study design is context, not a verdict.

A randomized trial and an observational study answer different questions and carry different limitations. HRD makes the design visible without pretending the label alone tells you whether a finding is true.

A
Systematic reviews & meta-analyses

Syntheses of multiple studies. Their usefulness depends on the quality, similarity, search process and completeness of the included evidence.

B
Randomized controlled trials

Random assignment can strengthen causal inference, but sample size, blinding, adherence, endpoints and attrition still matter.

C
Controlled / other clinical trials

Interventional human research that PubMed identifies as clinical or controlled but not necessarily randomized.

D
Observational research

Cohort, comparative and other observational designs can reveal associations, while confounding and selection effects can limit causal conclusions.

E
Reviews, case reports & other

Useful for mechanisms, hypotheses, unusual observations and context, but not automatically equivalent to controlled human outcome evidence.

Important: These are automated design buckets based primarily on PubMed publication-type metadata. They are not GRADE ratings, risk-of-bias reviews or clinical recommendations.
